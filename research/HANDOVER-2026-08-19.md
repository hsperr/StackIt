## 1. The one thing to understand

**The network is the ceiling, not the RL loop, and not the training signal.**

Trained the same 64x4 net by pure supervised imitation of AlphaBeta — a fixed,
non-moving target. Held-out top-1 agreement plateaued at **0.46**. Self-play RL,

It is **underfitting**, not overfitting: train policy loss 1.499 vs held-out

Everything below follows from that, and it retires the entire "the RL loop is

## 3. The architecture bench

`scripts/arch_bench.py` — swaps one thing at a time against the fixed supervised
target. ~7 min an arm on MPS, versus hours of self-play. **This is the tool; use
it before touching `alphazero/`.**

Stopping rule, set by the user: stop after 15 rounds without a 0.002 gain, **no
LR annealing**. Also by the user's instruction, the bench now prints TRAIN and
TEST loss/top-1 every report (re-scoring a fixed val-sized sample of the training
data with `eval_net`, not the optimiser's running average, so the two are the
same measurement on different data). Rationale: an LR drop bought the baseline 0.450 -> 0.467 and then
flattened. We are screening for something that changes the curve's *shape*, so an
arm that merely fine-tunes has failed.

| arm | params | best top-1 |
|---|---|---|
| `fc:64:4` (shipped head) | 323k | 0.4502 |
| `conv:64:4` (per-cell conv head) | 340k | 0.4647 |
| `conv:128:6` | 1,861k | **0.4647** |
| `fc:128:6` | 1,825k | 0.4499 |

Read it: **capacity does literally nothing.** Both heads land on the same number
at 5.7x the parameters -- 0.4647 twice, 0.4502 / 0.4499. Two flat levels
separated only by the head, which is worth +1.5pp and no more. A net that
underfits and does not improve when you make it 5.7x bigger is not
capacity-limited; it is limited by what its input carries, or by what one forward
pass can compute.

Results: `/tmp/stackit-handover-4/arch2/summary.json`, per-round
`arch2/<arm>/metrics.jsonl`, checkpoints `arch2/<arm>/best.pt`.

### The ceiling is 0.99, so the gap is real

`scripts/ab_selfagree.py`: AlphaBeta at 0.05s agrees with **itself** 99.0% of the
time (400 positions). The labels are essentially deterministic — there is no
noise floor hiding at 0.5. A perfect student scores ~0.99. We are at 0.46.

Second number from the same script: AlphaBeta 0.05s vs 0.3s agree only **80%**.
A fifth of the dataset is "the move you find in 50ms", not "the right move". If
a stronger teacher is ever wanted, the data must be regenerated.

### Not a pipeline bug

`scripts/memorize.py`: both heads memorise 190 positions (2 games) to **1.00
top-1 and 1.00 value sign in a single round**, and 2000 positions within 5. Zero
contradictory duplicate labels in the data. Encoder, targets, loss and optimiser
all work. The 0.46 ceiling is a generalisation failure, nothing else.

## 3b. Capacity is fine. It is a DATA problem. (measured after the bench)

The bench's arms all early-stopped on validation after ~2 epochs, so nothing had
ever been given the steps to fit its training set -- which made "5.7x the
parameters changes nothing" weaker than it looked. `scripts/memorize_scale.py`
removes validation from the stopping rule and trains until TRAIN top-1 stalls.

| n (no augmentation) | train top-1 | test top-1 |
|---|---|---|
| 2,000 | **1.0000** (2 rounds) | 0.17 |
| 20,000 | **0.9896** | 0.27 |
| 100,000 | 0.78 and still climbing | 0.37 (peaked at r20, then decayed) |
| 268,583 (augmented, from the bench) | 0.4748 | 0.4647 |

A 340k-parameter net memorises 20k positions almost perfectly. Capacity was never
the problem. `scripts/train_vs_val.py` confirms the other end: on the full set the
best `conv:64:4` scores 0.4748 train vs 0.4647 test -- a 1pp gap, so it is not
memorising the big set either, it is simply out of data.

**Test top-1 vs dataset size: 0.17 -> 0.27 -> 0.37 -> 0.46, still climbing, no
sign of flattening.** More teacher games is the clearest lever we have.

Caveat on the slope: the 268k row used 8-fold augmentation and the others did
not, so the direction is solid but the exact scaling is not. Taken at face value
it is roughly +0.15-0.20 per 10x data, which means reaching 0.9 by data alone
would need ~250x more (~750k games, ~100 h of generation). **Data helps and will
not finish the job**; it has to stack with canonicalisation (an effective 8x,
untested) and the conv head (+1.5pp, free).

### The corollary the user spotted: the self-play buffer is starved

`cfg.replay_capacity = 100000` counts **augmented** examples, so the buffer holds
**12,500 unique positions** and covers **3.1 iterations** of self-play
(`new_examples ~31,800`/iter = 3,976 unique from 200 games). On the table above
that is below the 20k row, i.e. self-play has spent its whole life in the most
data-starved part of the curve.

Fix: raise `replay_capacity` toward **2M augmented (= 250k unique)**. Caveats
before doing it blindly:
- a big FIXED buffer holds data from much weaker nets, and
  `az-1200steps-reverted` already measured stale targets hurting. KataGo grows
  its window with total data rather than pinning it -- prefer a growing window.
- steps/iter changes meaning: 400x128 is 0.51 epochs over 100k, 0.026 over 2M.
- the 4.7x positions-per-game gap vs AlphaBeta games (22.7 vs 94) is **resolved**:
  Playout Cap Randomization, working as designed. `pcr_prob=0.25` records only a
  quarter of main-net plies, and the 40% opponent-pool games record only the main
  net's half, giving `0.6*0.25 + 0.4*0.25*0.5 = 0.200` of plies. Games are not
  short. Residual: backing out the length gives ~113 plies while the one sampled
  `last_game.json` was 61 -- one sample, settle it with `scripts/game_len.py`.
- **budget the fill**: 22.7 unique/game x 200 games = 4,540 unique per iteration,
  so a 250k-unique window takes ~55 iterations to fill once. The cheaper co-lever
  is **`pcr_prob` 0.25 -> 0.5**, doubling recorded positions per game at the cost
  of self-play speed -- worth measuring now that data is the binding constraint.

Memory: `az-replay-buffer-starved`.

## 4. Canonical orientation — TESTED, IT LOSES

The user's idea. Ran it; it is worse, and the reason is instructive.

| arm | canonical, no aug | augmented baseline | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fc:64:4` | 0.4100 | 0.4502 | **-4.0pp** |
| `conv:64:4` | 0.4357 | 0.4647 | **-2.9pp** |

Final train/test gap: canonical **+0.075 / +0.093** vs augmented **~+0.010**, and
the canonical arms peak early (r23 / r36 vs r78 / r73) then overfit. Augmentation's
value was never the invariance lesson — it is the **8x rows** (2,148,268 vs
268,583) fed to a data-starved net. The label-conflict hazard was measured and is
irrelevant (0.29% of rows).

Worth exactly ONE retest now that ~2M positions exist: canonicalisation trades
data volume for an easier function, and that trade could flip when data is no
longer scarce. Do not adopt before that retest. Memory:
`az-canonical-orientation-loses`.

### The original reasoning, kept for the retest

The user's idea, and the best remaining candidate. Rotate every board into a
canonical member of its dihedral orbit, train only on those, and at inference
rotate in / ask the net / rotate the move back. Rotation invariance then holds by
construction instead of being something the net must learn — and it demonstrably
has not learned it (52% self-disagreement under rotation, memory
`az-symmetry-underfit`).

**Already built and validated.** `scripts/canon.py` writes
`/tmp/stackit-handover-4/ab_data/ab3000_canon.npz` (canonical = the dihedral
image whose encoded planes are lexicographically smallest; ties are byte-identical).

The one hazard was measured and is small: AlphaBeta breaks ties by move index, so
it is not rotation-equivariant, and canonicalising can merge rotations into
contradictory targets. Measured: 1,713 duplicate groups, 234 contradictory,
**830 rows = 0.29% of the dataset**. Not a blocker.

Note the collapse ratio is only **1.01x** — with 283k positions from random
openings almost no two are rotations of each other, so this does not shrink the
data. The expected win is that the net stops spending capacity on 8 orientations
of every pattern, which is the right kind of help for a net that underfits.

**To run it** (`arch_bench.py` argv[4]=0 disables augmentation, which is required
here — augmenting canonical data would put the 7 orientations back and leak val
symmetries into training):

```
mkdir -p /tmp/stackit-handover-4/arch_canon
python /tmp/stackit-handover-5/scripts/arch_bench.py \
    /tmp/stackit-handover-4/ab_data/ab3000_canon.npz \
    /tmp/stackit-handover-4/arch_canon "fc:64:4,conv:64:4" 0
```

Compare against 0.4502 / 0.4647. Ask the user first — battery.

### If canonicalisation also lands near +1.5pp

Then three independent structural changes have each bought ~1.5pp, and the
honest conclusion is that **the input encoding is the problem**: 9 binary planes
do not expose what a 7-ply search is reasoning about. Next suspects in order:

1. **Input planes.** `alphazero/encoding.py`, `NUM_PLANES=9`. A chip count near
   its explosion threshold is the whole game; check whether "cells one chip from
   exploding", "chain length if I play here", and liberty/neighbour-count planes
   are present. This is cheap to test in the bench.
2. **The task may be unlearnable in one forward pass.** AlphaBeta at 0.05s
   searches to depth 7. A 4-block convnet has a ~9x9 receptive field, which
   covers the board, but depth-7 explosion chains are a sequential computation.
   If so, the net is fine and the fix is more search at inference, not a better
   net — and the AlphaZero framing still works, it just needs a bar it can
   actually be measured against (section 2a).
3. Value head specifically: 0.66 sign accuracy has never moved across any arm or
   any run. Worth its own bench arm.

## 5. Things measured and closed — do not retry

- **More simulations in training** (from handover-4, still valid): saturates,
  search adds ~6 points over the prior.
- **Lowering `gumbel_c_scale` / `self_play_gumbel=False`**: `_sigma` already
  matches mctx exactly. Treat mctx as the arbiter for anything Gumbel.
- **More training steps per round**: 400 beats 1200.
- **Bigger network**: `conv:128:6` = `conv:64:4`, exactly.
- **Per-cell conv policy head alone**: +1.5pp. Real, not a fix.
- **LR annealing**: +1.7pp then flat. Real (8 sd above the 0.42pp binomial
  scatter floor on 14,135 val positions), but irrelevant at this scale.
- **A pipeline bug**: ruled out by the memorisation test.

## 5b. The one-line summary of the whole day

Four independent results all point the same way, and none of them is the thing
we spent three sessions chasing:

1. supervised imitation of a FIXED target plateaus where self-play plateaus

**Data is the binding constraint.** Everything else measured today was a rounding
error. And the self-play trainer holds 12,500 unique positions (section 3b),
which on curve (3) is worth about -0.2 top-1.

If the overnight run confirms scaling continues, the priority order is:
raise the replay buffer / `pcr_prob`, adopt the conv head (+1.5pp, 17k params),
and leave architecture alone.
